# Engraved Family Notes

## Outcome

Eight square 1254 x 1254 PNG plates were generated with the approved Patent Plate and Guilloche images used as style references only. Every final was opened at full size and inspected again at 264 x 264 through a circular mask. The review covered the requested anatomical relationships, muscle-fibre direction, attachment or compression site, crop safety, small-size legibility, colour restraint, and continued intaglio character.

No plate contains text, labels, arrows, people, faces, or hands. No final is release-blocking on visual QA. A Registered Physiotherapist should still give the final clinical-use approval, particularly where a non-specific condition has been converted into a single visual focus.

All inks are rich, saturated, and slightly desaturated toward antique printing. The paper remains warm ivory. Accent colour is localized to abnormal tissue rather than used as general decoration.

## Treatment fit

Patent Plate is strongest for layered or mechanically legible relationships: overlapping muscle planes, a tendon passing beneath an arch, insertion footprints, cartilage surfaces, joint-space comparison, and restrained cutaway anatomy. It particularly suits gluteal tendinopathy, rotator-cuff tear, posterior cervical layers, and knee osteoarthritis.

Guilloche is strongest when long fibre architecture or a continuous tissue path carries the subject: hamstring fibres, the extensor mass, calf-to-Achilles convergence, and the descending sciatic nerve. The rosette gives these plates greater collectible richness. Dense deep anatomy can merge at small size, so the nerve or lesion needs a tightly controlled contrasting accent.

## Strength assessment

Strongest overall: `hamstring-strain.png` for the clearest muscle-first statement and fibre-led Guilloche execution; `sciatica.png` for the richest colour, depth, and tissue contrast; `knee-osteoarthritis.png` for the most immediately legible Patent Plate pathology at 264 px.

Relative weakest: `neck-pain.png`. The anatomy and crop are strong, but "neck pain" is non-specific and does not supply one defensible structural lesion. The copper fibre focus is therefore a restrained visualization of unilateral muscular overload, not a diagnostic claim. If a future content decision makes the page explicitly non-specific, removing the accent would be more neutral.

## Final prompt set

The common production prompt was: square website condition-library plate; approved source images used as style references only; brand-new anatomical subject; commissioned fine intaglio engraving; swelling and tapering instrument lines; directional muscle or tendon hatching; warm ivory rag paper; faint construction geometry for Patent Plate or a quiet security rosette for Guilloche; single regional ink plus one localized pathology accent; centred within a circular safe area; legible at 264 px; no text, labels, arrows, people, faces, hands, logos, signatures, painting, neon, pastel, gore, or stock-medical styling.

Plate-specific prompts then fixed the anatomical view, named the required muscles or supporting landmarks, stated the correct fibre direction and origin/insertion relationship, defined the pathology morphology and location, and supplied the regional ink and accent values listed above. The two revision prompts changed only the rotator-cuff tear morphology and the Achilles scale plus tendon thickening.

Generation used the built-in image-generation mode.
